The problem

Every month, my mom group runs a workout and accountability challenge. We set goals, cheer each other on, complete mini missions, and compete for bragging rights. But the tracking was becoming a workout of its own.

We had tried proof threads, Google Sheets, Google Forms and a fitness app. Every new tool added another place to go—and another chance for people to drop off. Could we make the whole experience work inside Discord, where everyone already was?

What I built

With Claude helping on the technical side, I built a Discord bot that handles sign-ups and deadlines, three weekly goals per player, check-ins, photo sharing, daily reminders, mini missions, scores, leaderboards, team or solo mode, and weekly standings. Winners even get roles and crowned nicknames.

From rules to a working bot

On November 16 we started with the group's old rules and a simple question: what was making this harder than it needed to be? We worked through the scoring logic and built an initial working bot in roughly eight hours. On November 17, it went live in our group's server. We added more engagement features on November 18.

The most useful work came from watching what happened when people tried it. One friend got stuck entering her goals on a phone. We redesigned that flow four times, ending with a single form for all three goals. A player questioned why every goal was worth ten points. That started a bigger conversation about incentives, fairness, and the risk of someone giving up after one missed day. We landed on a simpler one-point-per-goal model with partial credit.

The AI helped build the software. The people using it helped make it better.

What I learned

  • Build where people already are. A new app was adding friction, not removing it.
  • Working is not the same as usable. The bot could accept goals before the goal-entry experience actually made sense on a phone.
  • AI makes iteration faster. Claude helped translate product decisions into code, tests, and deployment changes as we learned.
  • Judgment is still the work. We changed scoring because of how it might shape real behavior—not because the code failed.

What comes next

It's live in our private group. The next test is running a full challenge and seeing how the daily roundups, reminders and new flow work over time. I haven't measured whether it improves participation or retention yet.
