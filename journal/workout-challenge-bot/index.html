Building for My Toughest Users: My Mom Discord.

Our mom Discord has a monthly workout and accountability challenge: goals, check-ins, mini missions, friendly competition, and a lot of cheering each other on.

DISCORD BOT · PYTHON · LIVE IN PRIVATE GROUP · PUBLISHED NOVEMBER 18, 2026

The workout &
accountability

The problem

Every month, my mom Discord runs a workout and accountability challenge. We set goals, cheer each other on, complete mini missions, and compete for bragging rights. But the tracking was becoming a workout of its own.

We'd been running versions of the challenge for about a year, changing the rules as we learned what kept people engaged. We also tried Google Sheets, Google Forms, and a dedicated fitness app. Every new tool added another place to go, and another chance for people to drop off.

Could the whole thing live inside Discord, where everyone already was?

What I built

With Claude helping on the technical side, I built a Discord bot that runs the whole challenge:

  • Sign-ups with a deadline, and a daily reminder until they close
  • Three goals per player, entered in one form ("Yoga, 3 days")
  • Check-ins by posting a photo and tapping the goal, with an 8 p.m. reminder if you haven't checked in
  • A themed mini mission every morning: Munchie Monday, Work It Wednesday, Slow Down Sunday
  • A morning roundup with the leaderboard and everyone's progress on each goal
  • Team or solo play, depending on how many people sign up
  • Winners get a special role and a 👑 on their name until the next challenge

From changing rules to a working bot

On November 16, I brought the group's latest rules and a year of lessons to one question: what was making this harder than it needed to be? Claude asked questions first, helped me work through the scoring logic, and turned the answers into a working bot with tests that night. By the next afternoon it was running in our private server.

Since then, I've kept adding engagement features and refining the experience based on what the group tells me. One friend got stuck entering her goals on her phone. Goal entry went through four versions before landing on a single form for all three goals.

The scoring took four tries

Scoring turned out to be the hardest part, and none of it was a code problem.

  1. Our original rules gave a point for every completion. A daily goal like brushing your teeth earned more than three hard workouts a week.
  2. My first fix made every goal worth up to 10 points a week. It was fair, but nobody could tell where the 10 came from.
  3. Then I tried simple: 1 point for each goal you hit, nothing if you miss. The group pushed back fast. Miss Monday, and an all-or-nothing person gives up for the week. Others would set easy two-day goals so they couldn't fail.
  4. Where we landed: each goal is worth up to 1 point a week, and you get the share you hit. Two of three workouts is 0.67. Going past your target earns nothing extra. Hit all three and you get a bonus point.

Mini missions went through the same debate. Capping them kept them from outweighing goals, but the point of missions is to get people showing up every day. So there's no cap, each one is worth a little (0.25 or 0.5), and goals still decide the winner.

AI helped build the software. The people using it helped make it better.

What I learned

  • Build where people already are. Adding another app was creating friction instead of removing it.
  • Working isn't the same as usable. Goal entry worked technically before it made sense on a phone.
  • AI makes iteration faster. Claude turned product decisions into code, tests, and deployment updates as I learned, often within minutes of a piece of feedback.
  • Test with a fake month. A simulated month of players caught problems before anyone saw them, like a tie that crowned only one winner and a progress grid that showed almost everyone in green.
  • Judgment is still the work. Changing the scoring came from thinking about people and motivation, not fixing broken code.

What comes next

The bot is live in our private group. I'll keep improving it as we run challenges, and I'm watching how the reminders, check-ins, and simpler flows hold up over time. I haven't yet measured whether it improves participation or retention.
