Building for My Toughest Users: My Mom Discord.

After a year of changing rules and tracking tools, I built the workout challenge directly inside our mom Discord.

The problem

Every month, my mom Discord runs a workout and accountability challenge. We set goals, cheer each other on, complete mini missions, and compete for bragging rights. But the tracking was becoming a workout of its own.

We'd been running versions of the challenge for about a year, experimenting with the rules as we learned what kept people engaged. We also tried Google Sheets, Google Forms, and a dedicated fitness app. Every new tool added another place to go—and another opportunity for people to drop off.

Could we make the whole experience work inside Discord, where everyone already was?

What I built

With Claude helping on the technical side, I built a Discord bot that handles sign-ups and deadlines, three weekly goals per player, check-ins, photo sharing, daily reminders, mini missions, scores, leaderboards, team or solo play, and weekly standings. Winners even get special roles and crowned nicknames.

From changing rules to a working bot

On November 16, I brought the group's latest rules and a year of lessons into a new question: what was making this harder than it needed to be? Claude helped me work through scoring logic and turn the idea into a working bot. I had an initial version live in our private server that same day.

Since then, I've continued adding engagement features and refining the experience based on what the group tells me. One friend struggled to enter her goals on a phone. I reworked the goal-entry flow four times before landing on a single form for all three goals. Another challenged the way points were awarded, prompting a rethink of fairness, incentives, and how to avoid discouraging someone after one missed day. We moved toward simpler scoring with partial credit.

AI helped build the software. The people using it helped make it better.

What I learned

  • Build where people already are. Adding another app was creating friction instead of removing it.
  • Working isn't the same as usable. Goal entry worked technically before it made sense on a phone.
  • AI makes iteration faster. Claude helped turn product decisions into code, tests, and deployment updates as I learned.
  • Judgment is still the work. Changing the scoring came from thinking about people and motivation, not fixing broken code.

What comes next

The bot is live in our private group. I'm continuing to improve it as we run challenges, and I'm watching how the reminders, check-ins, and simplified flows work over time. I haven't yet measured whether it improves participation or retention.
