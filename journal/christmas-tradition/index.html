Can AI Help Me Save a 30-Year-Old Christmas Tradition?

One family. Three countries. A much-loved guessing game, and six weeks to see what I can build.

By Jenna Clark · November 2026
The experiment
Family & technology
Six-week build sprint

Project overview ↗

Every Christmas Day after dinner, my mother-in-law passes around her famous Argos game sheets.

For years, she's painstakingly cut pictures out of the iconic Argos catalogue, blacked out the prices with a Sharpie, and assembled them into a family guessing game. Closest to the actual price wins bragging rights and scratch-offs. Who doesn't love a little gambling on Christmas?

In 2020, Argos announced it was retiring its printed catalogue after 47 years and more than a billion copies. Thankfully, my mother-in-law had hoarded a backup stash. Crisis averted.

But we've had to get creative.

During the pandemic, she mailed the game sheets to us in New York so we could play together over the phone. Now I'm in Seattle, my brother-in-law is in Portugal, and the rest of the family is in the UK.

Three countries. Three time zones. One very determined mother-in-law.

So this year, I'm building a game.

The idea is to turn our family tradition into a live, interactive experience, with a shared screen displaying catalogue items and everyone submitting guesses from their phones, wherever they are.

The timing couldn't be better. Argos is revisiting the nostalgia of its iconic catalogues in its Christmas advertising. Apparently, I'm not the only one who isn't ready to let them go.

Here's where AI comes in.

I've spent years building teams, systems, and enterprise technology, often working alongside engineers to bring an idea to life. But I'm not a software developer.

AI is changing what's possible for people who know what they want to build but haven't traditionally written the code themselves.

AI can accelerate development, but it doesn't eliminate the need for sound architecture, testing, debugging, or judgment.

So I'm giving myself six weeks to see how far I can take this.

I'll be figuring out how to connect phones to a shared screen, experimenting with AI-assisted development, and undoubtedly breaking a few things along the way.

I'll share the process: the tools, the decisions, the mistakes, what works, and what doesn't.

Not another series about AI buzzwords. An actual project, with a real deadline and a family waiting to play it on Christmas Day.

Six weeks. One family tradition. Let's see what I can build.

This is a personal, independent project inspired by a family game using historic Argos catalogues. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Argos. The game is in development; no public playable version is promised yet.
